When you read a book, you get years of someone's thinking in a few hours, at your own pace. **You get the whole argument, with the reasoning and the doubts, and you can stop, go back and think it over before you agree or disagree.** Long conversations are better for getting to know how a person thinks, but for understanding an idea in depth, reading is hard to beat.

## The pace

You set it. A hard paragraph can be read three times, a chapter can wait until tomorrow, and you can argue with the author in the margin. With audio or video the speaker sets the speed, and it's hard to go back to a sentence you didn't follow.

## The depth

Some ideas need two hundred pages of groundwork before the point lands. A book has room for that, and for the objections and the places where the author isn't sure, which is usually what gets cut from a post or a clip. **An idea that holds up for a whole book is often stronger than one that fits in a paragraph.**

## The reach

Books let you learn from people you'll never meet, and from people who lived centuries ago. Their best thinking is still there, in the order they meant it to be read.

## The habit

Following one argument for hours is a skill, and like most skills it fades when you stop using it. A book is also one of the few things you still pick on purpose, while most of what we read on screens is chosen for us by a feed.

## Where listening wins

For getting a feel for how someone thinks, a long conversation does better (more in [Why listen to long conversations](/with-claude/why-listen-to-long-conversations/)).
